This page lists what Prince of Prints already does today, followed by planned upgrades — focused on real jobsite time savings: from offload to erection, locating tags fast, instant access to cut sheets, and reducing lost time on scrambled paperwork and prints (including damaged, covered, or missing tags).

A Personal Note from the Founder

With over 40 years of combined hands-on construction experience, our team has seen how much time is lost simply trying to locate steel that’s missing tags, mis-marked, or buried somewhere on site. These delays rarely get tracked, but they add up quickly. Just five ironworkers waiting 15 minutes for a single piece can quietly cost more than a full man-hour of productivity.

If the rigging foreman is called away, work does not stop. By color-coding upcoming pieces pink, a supervisor can instantly direct the ground crew’s next rigging sequence from anywhere. After sending a piece up for connection, the crew simply refreshes their device, and the next pink-marked members clearly indicate what to rig next.

With Prince of Prints, a rigger can click a pink-marked piece and instantly access the corresponding cut sheet through the click-and-fetch feature. This provides full length, web height, weight, connection details, and all associated notes. For example, if the piece is a 30-foot W12x40 beam, the rigger can quickly identify matching beams in the laydown yard and verify them even when tags are missing, damaged, or unreadable.

This application grew directly out of real field experience — including the realities of age, injury, and the need to continue contributing knowledge after hands-on work is no longer possible. At a minimum, it drastically reduces unnecessary trips back to the office or print shack, something anyone who has worked a muddy jobsite understands immediately.

When steel begins arriving and trucks stack up waiting to be off-loaded, a true inventory check almost never happens. Verifying every piece against a bill of lading or expected list simply isn’t realistic under real jobsite pressure. Material gets staged imperfectly, and problems surface later when time is most critical.

The app is intentionally straightforward. Enter a tag ID and it immediately jumps to that piece in the All list. Tap it — checked. Fast and simple. An expediter or forklift operator can stage material efficiently using nothing more than a tablet and a list of tags. If a piece cannot be located, it can be marked yellow directly on the erection drawing.

If a beam is sent up and turns out to be incorrect, locating the right piece can become a long and costly search. With instant access to cut sheet information, the crew immediately has the exact description and specifications needed to identify the correct member without walking, guessing, or losing valuable time.

Today, the application runs reliably on mobile phones and tablets and supports the core field workflow. Because every job’s print set is organized differently, the focus has been on practical automation — tools that generate indexes and streamline setup, while mapping remains a manual process for now.

Jobs can be configured for either internet access or fully offline use. Some crews prefer prints hosted online, while others require everything stored locally on a jobsite device or shared network. Prince of Prints supports both approaches, allowing each job to operate within its existing workflow.

A recently added feature—requested by field crews—is the Office Logistics section. It is organized into two access levels: a supervisor-locked area and a crew-facing area. Crew members can complete their individual daily logs and inspection sheets directly in the app, while supervisors review and sign using typed signature text.

The supervisor-controlled section includes time sheets, rework documentation, modification forms, back-charge logs, and personnel performance evaluations. Job Safety Analyses (JSAs) can also be completed within the system, with each crew member signing electronically on the supervisor’s mobile device during the required safety meeting to review the day’s planned work steps and safety considerations.

Completed records are delivered instantly to the office, eliminating unnecessary trips to the trailer and ensuring documentation is available in real time.

  • Mobile usability refinement — ongoing improvements for speed, touch targets, sunlight readability, and glove-friendly interaction.
  • Receiving & inventory workflows — optional checklist-based offload verification tied directly to tags.
  • Job-scoped synchronization — consistent status visibility across devices using the same job code.
  • Supervisor locks & permissions — role-based controls for status color management.
  • Automation tools — faster generation of indexes, mappings, and job setup artifacts.
  • Hardening & reliability — continued testing and performance tuning for real jobsite conditions.

Our goal is simple: reduce office traffic, reduce wasted man-hours, and give crews a faster way to verify what is on site and what is missing.
